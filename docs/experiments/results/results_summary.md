This research investigates epistemic-status distortion in AI-assisted Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI), focusing on whether Large Language Models (LLMs) transform explicitly unverified cyber-threat claims into statements that communicate stronger evidentiary certainty.

The study uses a controlled 2 × 2 experimental design to examine how variations in experimental conditions influence the way LLMs process and communicate uncertain threat information. An initial five-claim pilot is planned to assess the experimental procedure, dataset structure and evaluation criteria before the main study.

Generated outputs will be evaluated for uncertainty preservation, unsupported increases in certainty, changes in evidentiary language and the introduction of information not supported by the original claims.

Beyond the technical assessment, the research examines the implications of epistemic-status distortion for CTI reliability, security analyst decision-making, organisational threat assessments and cybersecurity governance.

The broader objective is to identify potential safeguards that preserve evidentiary status, improve traceability and support responsible AI integration into cyber-threat intelligence workflows.

Research Status: In Progress. Experimental findings and conclusions will be incorporated after data collection and evaluation.
