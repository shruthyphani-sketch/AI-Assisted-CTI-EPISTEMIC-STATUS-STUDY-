The proposed study has several limitations that must be considered when interpreting its findings.

Dataset Scope: The research uses a selected set of cyber-threat claims. The findings may not represent the full diversity of real-world Dark-Web threat intelligence.

Model Dependency: Outputs may vary across LLM providers, model versions and system configurations. Results from the tested models cannot automatically be generalised to all language models.

Prompt Sensitivity: Differences in prompt wording, system instructions and sampling parameters may influence generated responses and affect experimental outcomes.

Controlled Environment: The experiment evaluates model-generated outputs under controlled conditions. It does not fully reproduce the complexity of operational CTI environments, where analysts use multiple intelligence sources and verification processes.

Evaluation Subjectivity: Assessing whether a response has strengthened the evidentiary status of a claim may involve interpretative judgement. A predefined evaluation framework and consistent assessment procedure will be necessary to reduce subjectivity.

Limited Ground Truth: The study examines how models communicate the evidentiary status of supplied claims. It does not independently establish whether the underlying cyber-threat allegations are factually true.

Generalisability: Findings from the selected claims, experimental conditions and tested models should not be treated as universal conclusions about AI-assisted CTI.

These limitations will be documented throughout the research. Future work could expand the dataset, include additional models, introduce independent evaluators and examine the proposed safeguards in more realistic CTI workflows.
